**Award:** Free program; students work on unsolved problems in mathematics and applied mathematics (theoretical computer science, computational biology); mentored by MIT and university graduate students/faculty; final paper posted on PRIMES website and eligible for submission to national science competitions and professional journals.
**Eligibility:** High school juniors and sophomores residing in the United States outside Greater Boston. At least one of: USAMO/USAJMO qualifier, grade A in college-level proof-based math course, participation in summer math program with recommendation, or letter from college math professor (preferable but not required).

## Notes
Program operates year-round in four phases: Phase I (Jan 1–Pi Day, ~Mar 14): reading period; Phase II (Mar 14–Jun 15): active research; Phase III (Jun 15–Aug 31): independent study; Phase IV (Sep 1–Dec 31): write-up period. Juniors work on individual or group projects; sophomores on group projects. Mentors chosen by PRIMES or local mentors. PRIMES does not conflict with summer programs. Students from underrepresented groups and disadvantaged backgrounds encouraged to apply.
