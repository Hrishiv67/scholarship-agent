## Overview

Stanford FEST (Frosh Engineering STem, or similar; confirm exact name and program details at URL) is a diversity fly-in hosted by Stanford's School of Engineering for high school juniors from underrepresented backgrounds. The multi-day program includes lab tours, faculty and student panels, design challenges, residential stays, and financial aid sessions. Stanford Engineering is among the most selective programs in the world — its CS, Electrical Engineering, Aeronautics and Astronautics (Aero/Astro), and Bioengineering programs are consistently top-ranked. The Stanford campus in Palo Alto sits at the heart of Silicon Valley, and undergraduate students have direct access to one of the most active startup and research ecosystems anywhere. Stanford's financial aid is extremely generous: families earning under $150,000/year typically pay $0 in tuition, and middle-income families receive significant grant support.

## CRITICAL: Eligibility Note — South Asian Applicants

**South Asian eligibility for Stanford FEST is explicitly uncertain and must be verified before any application effort.** Stanford Engineering's equity initiatives have historically focused on groups underrepresented in engineering — primarily Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, and Pacific Islander students, along with women and first-generation/low-income students. South Asian students are generally **not** considered underrepresented in engineering at Stanford, where they make up a substantial portion of the student body.

Before investing any time in the FEST application, Hrishiv should:
1. Read the eligibility page at the URL above word-for-word
2. If unclear, email Stanford Engineering directly (eng-diversity@stanford.edu or the admissions office) to ask whether a South Asian male student qualifies
3. Do not apply if the program is restricted to groups that do not include South Asian

This is one of the programs on this list where eligibility is most in question. Do not assume eligibility based on the program's general "diversity" framing.

## Requirements

Typical application components include:
- Short essays on interest in engineering, personal background, and/or community (often 2–3 prompts)
- Unofficial transcript
- Activities and honors list
- Junior enrollment confirmation
- Demographic information

## What to Highlight in Application (if eligible)

- **Published ML research (NC State)** — Stanford's CS and AI programs are world-leading; ML research published as a 10th grader is a compelling credential
- **TARC 2nd nationally** — directly relevant to Stanford's Aeronautics and Astronautics department, one of the top Aero/Astro programs in the country
- **VEX Robotics top 150 worldwide** — robotics achievement aligns with Stanford's extensive robotics research programs
- **Duke BME research** — Stanford Bioengineering is top-ranked; prior BME research demonstrates genuine interest and capability
- **Books for Africa** — Stanford's culture values "having an impact at scale"; $85k raised for African education is a compelling demonstration of this
- Be specific: mention Stanford labs or faculty whose work you have read (e.g., the Heer Lab, the Robotics group, the BioX interdisciplinary program)
