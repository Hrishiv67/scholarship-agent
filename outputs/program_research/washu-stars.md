# WashU STARS Program

**URL:** https://admissions.wustl.edu/discover/diversity/
**Application Opens:** September to early October (estimated)
**Deadline:** ~November 1 (estimated; verify at URL above)
**Award:** All-expenses campus visit to St. Louis, MO (flights, hotel, meals)
**Tier:** Competitive
**Eligibility:** Underrepresented high school juniors; WashU typically includes underrepresented racial/ethnic minorities, first-generation college students, and students with financial need
**Last Verified:** 2026-08-08

---

## Overview

WashU STARS (Students and Alumni Reaching for Stars, or similar; confirm name on current website) is Washington University in St. Louis's diversity fly-in program for high school juniors. The multi-day program includes academic exploration sessions, lab visits, student panels, overnight stays in WashU dorms, and detailed financial aid workshops. WashU is a top-20 research university with exceptional programs in biomedical engineering (often ranked in the top 5 nationally), computer science, and pre-medical tracks. Its McKelvey School of Engineering is small, well-funded, and highly research-intensive. WashU is also known for substantial merit scholarships in addition to need-based aid, which is a meaningful consideration.

## Eligibility Note — South Asian Applicants

WashU's diversity fly-in programs have historically targeted students from underrepresented racial/ethnic groups (Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, Pacific Islander) and first-generation college students. South Asian students are generally **not** considered underrepresented at WashU under the standard definition. **Hrishiv must verify eligibility on the admissions diversity page before applying.** WashU sometimes runs separate programs or has broader eligibility criteria that include socioeconomic background — it is worth checking each listed program individually. If first-generation status or financial need qualifies independently, that is worth pursuing.

## Requirements

Typical application components include:
- Short essay responses (often about interest in a specific field and personal background)
- Unofficial transcript / GPA
- Activities list
- Junior enrollment confirmation
- Parent/guardian educational background

## What to Highlight in Application

- **Duke BME research** — WashU has one of the top BME programs in the country; prior BME research experience is a strong and direct signal of fit
- **Published ML research (NC State)** — computational approaches in biomedicine are a major WashU research theme; this is highly relevant
- **TARC 2nd nationally + VEX Robotics** — engineering excellence; relevant to McKelvey School
- **Books for Africa ($85k)** — WashU's admissions values community leadership and service; the scale of this project is memorable
- Frame interest in WashU's interdisciplinary approach to biomedical engineering (they often collaborate with Washington University School of Medicine, one of the top medical schools in the country)

## Recommended Prep Timeline

- **September:** Check the diversity page; read eligibility for each listed program
- **Early-to-mid October:** Begin essay drafts if eligible
- **Late October:** Finalize; have counselor review
- **By October 28:** Submit before November 1

## Notes

WashU's location in St. Louis is an underrated asset — St. Louis has a low cost of living, a strong medical and biotech corridor, and the nearby Barnes-Jewish Hospital / BJC HealthCare complex that WashU students regularly access for research. The school is sometimes overlooked because it is not in a major coastal city, but academic outcomes (medical school placement, research output, employment in STEM) are comparable to peer Ivy-adjacent schools. Merit scholarships at WashU can be substantial even for middle-income families, making the financial aid session genuinely worth attending. WashU also has a general Multicultural Campus Preview that may have broader eligibility than the STARS-specific fly-in.
