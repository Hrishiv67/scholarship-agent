# Princeton Diversity Open House

**URL:** https://admission.princeton.edu/discover-princeton/programs-prospective-students
**Application Opens:** Mid-September to early October (estimated)
**Deadline:** ~November 1 (estimated; verify at URL above)
**Award:** All-expenses campus visit to Princeton, NJ (flights, hotel, meals)
**Tier:** Highly Competitive
**Eligibility:** Underrepresented high school juniors; Princeton typically includes Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, Pacific Islander, and first-generation college students
**Last Verified:** 2026-08-08

---

## Overview

Princeton's Diversity Open House (sometimes called "Diversity Open House" or listed under various program names on the admissions page) is a multi-day fly-in for high school juniors from underrepresented backgrounds. Participants attend academic sessions and seminars, meet current students, experience residential life in the iconic dormitories, and receive detailed financial aid information. Princeton has the most generous financial aid program among Ivy League schools — it meets 100% of demonstrated need with grants (no loans), a fact that makes this visit especially valuable even as an informational experience. Princeton's engineering school (SEAS) covers operations research, CS, electrical engineering, and has strong interdisciplinary connections to the Lewis Center.

## Eligibility Note — South Asian Applicants

Princeton's diversity programs have historically been aimed at students from groups underrepresented in higher education. The most commonly listed groups are Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, and Pacific Islander students, along with first-generation college students. South Asian students are **generally not** included in the standard underrepresented definition at Princeton. **Hrishiv must verify eligibility directly on the admissions page before applying.** If Princeton uses a broader definition — for example, including students who would be first in their family to attend a US university, or students with significant financial need — there may be a path. Emailing Princeton admissions (undergrad.admission@princeton.edu) to ask directly is a legitimate and appropriate step.

## Requirements

Typical application components include:
- Short personal essays or responses (often 2–3 prompts, ~200 words each)
- Unofficial transcript and GPA
- Extracurricular activities list
- Confirmation of current junior enrollment
- Parent/guardian educational background

## What to Highlight in Application

- **Published ML research (NC State)** — Princeton values intellectual ambition above almost anything else; original research published as a 10th grader signals exactly that
- **TARC 2nd nationally** — Princeton SEAS has strong aerospace connections; national-level rocketry achievement resonates
- **VEX Robotics top 150 worldwide** — sustained excellence in a global competition
- **Duke BME research** — Princeton is building out its bioengineering offerings; this is relevant
- **Books for Africa** — Princeton has a strong culture of service and civic commitment; $85k raised is extraordinary for a high school student
- DECA ICDC adds breadth and demonstrates ability to think across domains

## Recommended Prep Timeline

- **Mid-September:** Check the URL; confirm whether the application is live and read eligibility carefully
- **Early October:** Begin essay drafts — Princeton prompts tend to ask about community, identity, or intellectual passion
- **Mid-to-late October:** Finalize applications; get counselor review
- **By October 28:** Submit ahead of November 1

## Notes

Princeton is known for its eating clubs, concentrated residential campus, and exceptional undergraduate research opportunities (PRISM, senior thesis requirement). Even if the fly-in does not work out, Princeton hosts general campus preview days throughout the fall that any prospective student can attend. The financial aid information alone makes any campus visit worthwhile — Princeton regularly meets 100% of need with grants, meaning families earning under ~$100k often pay $0. This context makes a strong application a high-priority effort.
