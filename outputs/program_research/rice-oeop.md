# Rice OEOP Fly-In (Office of Enrollment and Academic Programs)

**URL:** https://oeop.rice.edu/
**Application Opens:** September to early October (estimated)
**Deadline:** ~November 1 (estimated; verify at URL above)
**Award:** All-expenses campus visit to Houston, TX (flights, hotel, meals)
**Tier:** Competitive
**Eligibility:** Underrepresented high school juniors; Rice OEOP typically includes students from underrepresented racial/ethnic backgrounds, first-generation college students, and students from low-income households
**Last Verified:** 2026-08-08

---

## Overview

Rice University's OEOP (Office of Enrollment and Academic Programs) runs multiple fly-in and diversity programming initiatives for high school students. The campus visit component typically brings juniors to Houston for two to three days, with activities including academic department tours, student panels, faculty interactions, residential college tours (Rice's residential college system is a distinguishing feature), and financial aid sessions. Rice punches above its weight academically: it is a top-20 research university with a small undergraduate enrollment (~4,000 students), and its engineering programs — particularly bioengineering, CS, and electrical engineering — are excellent. Rice's proximity to NASA's Johnson Space Center is a direct connection to aerospace interests.

## Eligibility Note — South Asian Applicants

Rice OEOP's fly-in programs have historically been designed for students from groups underrepresented in higher education. The commonly listed groups are Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, and Pacific Islander students, along with first-generation college students. South Asian students are typically **not** included in Rice's standard underrepresented definition. **Hrishiv must check the OEOP eligibility page carefully before applying.** Rice OEOP runs several distinct programs (some targeted at specific groups), so it is worth reading each program's description individually to find the right fit. If first-generation college student status or financial need independently qualifies, that could open a path.

## Requirements

Typical application components include:
- Short-answer essays (often asking about interest in engineering/STEM and personal background)
- Unofficial transcript
- Activities and honors list
- Junior enrollment confirmation
- Parent/guardian information (particularly educational background for first-gen consideration)

## What to Highlight in Application

- **NASA/aerospace connection:** Rice's proximity to Johnson Space Center is a major asset; TARC 2nd nationally and VEX Robotics are natural hooks here
- **Published ML research (NC State)** — demonstrates research readiness that fits Rice's research-intensive culture
- **Duke BME research** — relevant to Rice's Department of Bioengineering, which is highly regarded
- **DECA ICDC** — Rice's Brown School of Engineering values entrepreneurial thinking; business competition experience adds depth
- **Books for Africa** — community impact at scale shows leadership and values that resonate with Rice's service culture
- Mention Rice's residential college system explicitly — it shows you understand what makes Rice distinctive

## Recommended Prep Timeline

- **September:** Check oeop.rice.edu for active programs and opening dates; read each program's eligibility criteria
- **Early-to-mid October:** Begin application; note that OEOP sometimes has a rolling process
- **Late October:** Finalize and submit
- **By October 28:** Aim to submit before November 1

## Notes

Houston is a diverse, large city with a significant South Asian community. Rice is genuinely small and intimate — average class sizes are smaller than most Ivies — and the student culture is reported to be collaborative rather than cutthroat. The NASA partnership means there are undergraduate research opportunities in aerospace and earth science that are difficult to find elsewhere. If the OEOP fly-in eligibility does not apply, Rice offers its own general open houses and information sessions throughout the fall. Rice's financial aid is good but not as generous as the most elite schools; this makes understanding the financial aid session particularly valuable.
