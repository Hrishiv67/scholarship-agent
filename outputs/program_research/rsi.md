## Overview
RSI is the most prestigious high school science research program in the United States, hosted at MIT in Cambridge, Massachusetts each summer. Selected students — roughly 80 domestic and 20-40 international — spend 6 weeks conducting original mentored research with faculty from MIT, Harvard, and other Boston-area universities. The program is entirely free; CEE covers housing, meals, travel, and all costs. Alumni include Terence Tao (Fields Medal), Feng Zhang (CRISPR pioneer), and co-founders of Pinterest, Perplexity AI, and Cursor.

## Requirements
- Online application at cee.org (takes ~1 hour to complete)
- Official high school transcripts
- SAT or ACT scores (PSAT scores also accepted; 740+ math / 700+ EBRW PSAT recommended)
- Two required recommendation letters (math or science teacher, or research supervisor) + one optional additional rec
- Multiple short essay responses (prompts vary annually — typically 3-5 essays)
- Description of prior research experience
- Research interest statement / proposed area of study
- No application fee

## Essay Prompts / Application Questions
Not yet posted for 2026-2027 — check https://www.cee.org/rsi-application in October 2026. Prior year themes have included:
1. Describe a scientific problem you want to solve and why it matters
2. Describe your most meaningful research or scientific experience
3. What scientific discovery or paper has most influenced your thinking?
4. Describe a time you failed at something and what you learned
5. What would you research if accepted to RSI?

Prompts vary each cycle. Treat them like college supplemental essays: concrete, specific, and voice-forward.

## What a Strong Application Looks Like
- **Prior independent research is near-essential** — most admitted students have conducted mentored research with a university faculty member, not just class projects
- Strong academic record: top 5% of class; SAT 1500+ is the competitive sweet spot, though exceptional research can compensate for lower scores
- STEM competition achievements (Regeneron STS finalist, ISEF, USAMO, Putnam) are common on admitted applications
- Published or presented work (even preprints like Zenodo) is a differentiator — relatively rare at the high school level
- Essays that convey deep intellectual curiosity, not just a list of accomplishments; show what questions you actually lose sleep over
- Specific, feasible research proposal that demonstrates you understand what it means to do real science
- Recommendation letters from research supervisors who can speak to your ability to do independent work — teacher recs are secondary
