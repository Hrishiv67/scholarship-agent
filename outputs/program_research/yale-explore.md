# Yale EXPLORE Diversity Fly-In

**URL:** https://admissions.yale.edu/diversity-programs
**Application Opens:** Mid-September to early October (estimated)
**Deadline:** ~November 1 (estimated; verify at URL above)
**Award:** All-expenses 2-day campus visit to New Haven, CT (flights, hotel, meals)
**Tier:** Highly Competitive
**Eligibility:** Underrepresented high school juniors; Yale typically defines this as students from racial/ethnic groups underrepresented in higher education, first-generation college students, and students with financial need
**Last Verified:** 2026-08-08

---

## Overview

Yale EXPLORE is Yale University's flagship diversity fly-in for high school juniors who may not otherwise visit an elite university campus. The two-day program includes academic panels, faculty Q&A, residential life immersion (students stay with current Yale undergrads), financial aid sessions, and campus tours. Yale's intimate residential college system, liberal arts approach, and top-tier programs across STEM and humanities make it a distinctive campus experience. For STEM students, Yale's CS and biomedical programs are growing rapidly, and the Tsai City innovation hub and West Campus research facilities are notable draws.

## Eligibility Note — South Asian Applicants

Yale EXPLORE has traditionally focused on students from groups that are statistically underrepresented in higher education — most commonly Black, Hispanic/Latino, Native American, and Pacific Islander students, as well as first-generation college students. South Asian students are generally **not** considered underrepresented under standard higher education definitions. **This is one of the most important eligibility checks before applying.** Hrishiv should read Yale's EXPLORE eligibility page word-for-word and, if unclear, email Yale admissions directly to ask. Some Yale diversity programs use a broader definition that includes socioeconomic background; if first-generation or financial need qualifies, that may open the door. Do not assume eligibility without confirming.

## Requirements

Typical application components include:
- Short personal statement (often ~300 words) on background, interests, or a specific prompt
- Unofficial transcript
- Extracurricular activities list
- Confirmation of junior year enrollment
- Demographic information
- Parent/guardian educational background (for first-gen verification)

## What to Highlight in Application

- **Published ML research (NC State / Zenodo)** — publishable research as a 10th grader is genuinely rare and will stand out even in a Yale applicant pool
- **Duke BME research** — relevant to Yale's biomedical and biochemistry programs
- **TARC 2nd nationally** — engineering excellence at the national level
- **VEX Robotics top 150 worldwide** — sustained competitive technical achievement
- **Books for Africa ($85k)** — community leadership at a remarkable scale for a high schooler
- Articulate intellectual curiosity and interest in interdisciplinary study, which aligns with Yale's liberal arts STEM approach

## Recommended Prep Timeline

- **Mid-September:** Check if the EXPLORE application is live; confirm eligibility
- **Late September:** Draft personal statement; Yale prompts often ask about your community, identity, or a challenge
- **Mid-October:** Finalize and review; get feedback from a counselor
- **By October 28:** Submit ahead of the November 1 deadline

## Notes

Yale EXPLORE is among the most selective fly-in programs — typically 100–150 students are selected nationally. The program fills quickly once invitations go out. If selected, the residential stay (sleeping in a Yale dorm with a current student host) is a uniquely immersive experience. Even if EXPLORE eligibility is unclear, Yale's Open Campus events and general information sessions in October and November are worth attending. New Haven is also a reasonable drive from the Raleigh-Durham area (roughly 13 hours) or a short flight, so self-funded visits remain an option.
